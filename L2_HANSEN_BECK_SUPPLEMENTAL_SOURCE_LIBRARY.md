## Why These Sources Are Included

Recurring Hansen Beck development chain: **Vision/mindset → knowledge/skill → deliberate behavior → influence → results → reflection/repetition.** This sits *underneath* the Launch to Lead Magic Model (Next-Level Work → Known for Impact → Trusted by Seniors) as a source of supporting principles and exercises — it should never become a competing framework.

- Personal effectiveness and project management strengthen **Next-Level Work**.
- Presentation, customer-centered thinking, and value communication strengthen **Known for Impact**.
- Leadership, management conversations, negotiation, and influence strengthen **Trusted by Seniors**.

### Program-Design Principle

Each mission can be designed through three layers: **Mindset** (what belief must change) → **Model** (what must the engineer understand) → **Behavior** (what must they visibly do at work). Recurring mission cycle: **See it → Define it → Plan it → Practice it → Prove it → Improve it.**

## Application to the Current Magic Model

### Next-Level Work
- **Master Workload:** habit design, prioritizing "big stones," decision-making, work breakdown, dependencies, risk recognition. Trust signal: *my manager doesn't need to carry my workload mentally.*
- **Train Others:** motivation/capability/environment, tailored coaching conversations, adapting support to the learner's competence/confidence. Completion evidence = another engineer became independently capable.
- **Expand Scope:** project definition, stakeholder mapping, work packages, milestones, change management, formal closeout — an assignment with real ambiguity, multiple stakeholders, and a clear decision/handoff.

### Known for Impact
- **Demonstrate Value:** audience-centered presentation (core message, desired feeling/action), paired with the Impact Bullet Builder — the IBB defines *what* the evidence contains, presentation concepts define *how* it's communicated.
- **Spread Impact:** knowledge transfer, reusable documentation, operational handoff, creating active advocates.
- **Reach Leaders:** stakeholder-centered journeys, genuine curiosity, open questions, active listening, silence, confirmation, action-oriented conversations.

### Trusted by Seniors
- **Meet Commitments:** clear scope, feasibility, milestones, risk monitoring, Go/Stop/Loop decisions.
- **Own Outcomes:** choosing to act, comparing current/desired state, full-picture responsibility, corrective action.
- **Influence Others:** assumptions, subconscious threat/security, emotional preparation, curiosity, silence, active listening, collaborative negotiation, explicit commitment.

## Cross-Program Applications

- **Build Sprint:** create awareness, learn the model, prepare tools, practice, plan application.
- **Application Sprint:** apply the behavior under real conditions, collect feedback, adjust, repeat, preserve evidence.
- **Mission completion standard:** one real workplace situation + one deliberate behavior + one observable outcome + one stakeholder response + one piece of promotion evidence captured + one improvement identified.
- **Impact closeout questions** (useful for Demonstrate Value / Spread Impact / Reach Leaders and the participant's evidence file): What problem existed? What changed? What was my specific contribution? What measurable result occurred? Who benefited? What was adopted/transferred/reusable? What did we learn? Who needs to understand the outcome? What's the next opportunity?

## Strong Social Content Territories (translate, don't copy)

1. Technical skill ≠ next-level readiness — value shifts from individual problem-solving to outcomes through systems and people (Train Others, Expand Scope, Influence Others).
2. Knowledge ≠ promotion evidence — evidence is observable behavior under real workplace pressure (all nine missions, especially Application Sprint messaging).
3. Managers shouldn't have to carry our workload mentally — trust grows from clarifying commitments and surfacing risk early (Master Workload, Meet Commitments).
4. Training ≠ explaining once — proof is independent performance after support is removed (Train Others).
5. Visibility = stakeholder relevance, not social exposure — start from a leader's priorities, not generic networking (Reach Leaders).
6. A "completed" project can still be invisible — value isn't real until it's adopted, documented, and communicated (Demonstrate Value, Spread Impact).
7. Influence begins before the argument — assumptions, trust, and listening determine whether people engage with the logic at all (Influence Others).
8. Silence can be a leadership behavior — a deliberate pause creates space for ownership (Influence Others, Train Others, Reach Leaders).
9. Owning a task ≠ owning the outcome — outcome ownership includes adoption, dependencies, correction, follow-through (Own Outcomes).
10. Planning isn't certainty — it lets us detect change early enough to respond intelligently (Master Workload, Expand Scope, Meet Commitments).

## Hansen Beck Concept Card Library (summary — 21 cards across 3 pages)

Cards translate into Launch to Lead missions as follows (full card-by-card detail — front/back text, exact prompts, and evidence cautions on unverifiable statistics like the 7/38/55 communication split or facial-muscle counts — should be treated as directional only, not quoted publicly without verification):

1. **Preparation** → Master Workload, Meet Commitments, Reach Leaders, Influence Others (plan the outcome, anticipate risk, rehearse the message).
2. **Visions Determine Our Behaviour** → opening framing for the Blueprint (define the promotion-ready destination and its evidence).
3. **Personal Responsibility** → Own Outcomes, Expand Scope, Meet Commitments ("we can't control the promotion decision, but we can control whether our manager has a strong case").
4. **Setting Objectives (SMART)** → separate the outcome goal ("get promoted") from weekly behavioral goals (create evidence, hold a conversation, lead a defined initiative).
5. **Perception** → Demonstrate Value, Reach Leaders, Influence Others (check what was actually understood, not just what was said).
6. **First Contact** → Reach Leaders, Influence Others (prepare, connect, ask open questions, listen).
7. **Positive Attitude** → preparation prompt before high-stakes conversations — not forced positivity, not a substitute for real problem-solving.
8. **Influential Presentations** → Demonstrate Value, Spread Impact, Reach Leaders (define the desired audience action first, then choose evidence/delivery).
9. **Addressing the Uncomfortable** → Influence Others, Train Others, Own Outcomes (direct feedback + permission + facts + empathy + silence + shared problem-solving + commitment).
10. **Communication & Negotiation** → Reach Leaders, Influence Others (questions, reasons, listening, silence, relevance — the longest explanation doesn't necessarily lead the conversation).
11. **Calling** → stakeholder outreach/follow-up (deliberate opening, relevant middle, explicit next step).
12. **Creating the Written Offer** → Expand Scope proposals, leadership recommendations, Launch to Lead's own sales materials (recipient should recognize their own priorities in it).
13. **Fully Active Recommendation** → Spread Impact, proof/testimonial strategy (strongest signal: leaders start mentioning your name in rooms you're not in).
14. **Closing the Sale** → Influence Others, Reach Leaders, manager conversations, Launch to Lead enrollment (finish with an explicit owner/action/date/decision).
15. **Price Negotiation** → primarily Launch to Lead sales; parallel internal use is negotiating scope/time/resources without apologizing for legitimate requirements.
16. **Added Value** → Demonstrate Value, offer communication ("I worked hard" isn't value — what became faster, safer, cheaper, clearer, more scalable?).
17. **Complaints** → Own Outcomes, Meet Commitments, Influence Others (senior trust is built by how we respond when something goes wrong, not by never having a problem).
18. **Influencing Outwards** → Reach Leaders, Spread Impact, Expand Scope, Influence Others (bring a business case, not just a request for visibility).
19. **Equal Communication** → Influence Others, Reach Leaders, Train Others, speaking up about risk ("equal" = professional dignity and candor, not denial of hierarchy).
20. **Personal Effectiveness** → Master Workload, Expand Scope ("being busy can prevent the very work that would make us promotion-ready").
21. **Our Growing Mind** → Application Sprint, retrospectives, coaching posture (promotion readiness includes proving we can learn and adapt, not that we already know everything).

## Card-Derived Cross-Program Practices

1. Prepare both the work and the internal posture.
2. Convert outcome wishes into behavioral objectives.
3. Design communication around reception and action, not just explanation completeness.
4. Use questions, silence, and listening as leadership tools.
5. End conversations with commitments (owner, action, date, decision).
6. Treat problems as tests of ownership.
7. Focus on stakeholder value in proposals and presentations.
8. Create advocacy, not just awareness.
9. Protect the "big stones" from routine busyness.
10. Build a growth-and-application loop (practice → feedback → correction → repetition).
