A 6-month group program for engineers who are doing good work but aren't yet clearly demonstrating that they're ready for the next level.

The program focuses on three things:

  • Getting involved in higher-impact work
  • Making your contributions visible to the people who influence promotion decisions
  • Building evidence that you can handle more responsibility, ownership, and leadership
Who This Is For

This is for engineers who are performing well but feel stuck at their current level.

You may be getting good feedback, completing your work, and doing what is expected of you, but still feel unclear about what you need to do differently to move forward.

The goal is to help you understand what is missing and build a practical plan to address it in your actual job.

What We Work On

Getting higher-impact work

We look at the work you are doing today and where you need to demonstrate stronger ownership, broader scope, or more responsibility.

You'll work on things like:

  • Becoming consistently reliable in your current responsibilities
  • Identifying work that better demonstrates next-level capability
  • Taking ownership beyond your normal assignments
  • Having better conversations with your manager about scope and responsibility

Making your impact visible

Doing good work is important, but the people involved in promotion decisions also need evidence of that work and its impact.

You'll work on:

  • Clearly explaining the impact of your work
  • Sharing results without unnecessary self-promotion
  • Building visibility through real work
  • Creating opportunities to present, document, or share useful work with other teams and leaders

Building evidence that you can operate at the next level

The goal is to build real examples of you taking on more responsibility before you ask someone to promote you into it.

That can include:

  • Owning problems instead of only completing assigned tasks
  • Training or supporting other engineers
  • Improving processes or documentation
  • Leading parts of projects
  • Working across teams
  • Influencing decisions
  • Following through on commitments with less oversight
How The Program Works

The program lasts 6 months and is split into two parts.

First 3 months: Learn and build

We meet as a group every week. Each week, we cover a specific concept related to becoming more promotion-ready and how to apply it in your job. You'll have specific actions to work on between calls.

The group calls also give you a chance to bring in situations you are dealing with at work, ask questions, and learn from what other engineers in the group are experiencing.

Next 3 months: Put it into practice

The second half is focused on applying what you learned to your actual job. This gives you time to pursue better work, build stronger examples of impact, improve how you communicate with your manager and leaders, and start building real evidence instead of just learning concepts.

The entire program is designed around applying this work to your current career while you are in the program.

Why This Is A Group Program

This is intentionally a group program rather than 1-on-1.

Your career situation is unique, but many of the problems engineers run into are not. Someone else may ask a question you hadn't thought to ask. You may see how another engineer handled a difficult manager, found higher-impact work, communicated their results, or dealt with a promotion conversation.

You get to learn from those situations instead of only learning from your own. You also have a group of engineers working toward similar goals who can share experiences, ideas, and feedback.

And when the program ends, you don't just walk away having worked with me. You have relationships with other engineers you can continue to lean on as your careers progress.

What You Should Have By The End

The program does not guarantee that your employer will promote you. Your company, manager, available positions, business conditions, and other factors are outside your control. What we can work on are the things you can control.

By the end of the program, the goal is for you to have:

  • A clearer understanding of what your next level requires
  • Stronger examples of higher-level work
  • Better evidence of your impact
  • More visibility with the right people
  • More examples of ownership and leadership
  • A stronger case to bring into promotion conversations
  • A repeatable way to manage your career going forward

Frequently Asked Questions

Why isn't this 1-on-1?

Because I think the group format makes the program better. In a 1-on-1 conversation, you only get to learn from your own questions and situations. In a group, you also get to hear the questions, experiences, problems, and ideas of other engineers working through many of the same career challenges. You may learn just as much from a situation another engineer brings to the group as you do from your own. It also means you have a community to lean on instead of relying entirely on me, and those relationships don't have to end when the program does.

Does this guarantee I'll get promoted?

No. Nobody outside your company can honestly guarantee that. Promotion decisions depend on your performance, your manager, company needs, available positions, timing, and other factors. The purpose of the program is to help you improve the things you can control and build a much stronger case for being ready for the next level.

What if I'm already doing well at my job?

That's who this is primarily for. Doing your current job well is important, but promotion usually requires evidence that you can operate beyond the expectations of your current level. The program focuses on helping you identify and build that evidence.
